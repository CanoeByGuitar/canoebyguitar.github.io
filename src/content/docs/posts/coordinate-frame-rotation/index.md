## Problem

Let the original frame be \(\mathcal F=(x,y,z)\). A second frame \(\mathcal F'\) is obtained by rotating the basis through a rotation matrix \(R\).

The geometric point \(A\) stays fixed in world space. If its old coordinates are

$$
a=\begin{bmatrix}2\1\1\end{bmatrix},
$$

then its coordinates in the rotated frame are

$$
a'=R^Ta.
$$

The demo uses a \(60^\circ\) rotation about the \(z\)-axis.
